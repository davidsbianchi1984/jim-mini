**JIM-mini v0.23.0 — the ninety seconds belonged to somebody who could not
reach them.**

`relay_guidance` states its audience in one sentence: *"Public: the person
standing over a colleague has no account and needs an answer in ninety
seconds."*

Its console binding sent a credential, so a route written for somebody with no
account could only be called by somebody with one. Its only caller was the
Guardian's own screen — the person at a desk deciding whether to escalate, not
the person on the floor. And the surface the passer-by actually reaches, the
page a camera app opens on scanning a sticker, raised the alarm, showed the
Medical ID, and stopped.

**The guidance box is now on that page**, built from the alarm id the alarm's
own response returns. It renders whether or not the Medical ID opened — a
minor's beacon opens no clinical stage to anybody, and the person kneeling over
a child needs to know what to do more than anyone, not less.

**And that page speaks the finder's language.** Every localization path here
takes a `user_id`; this reader has none, so they got English everywhere in the
world — including the line telling them to call an ambulance and the
instruction not to move the person. `Accept-Language` rides on every one of
those requests and nothing read it. Forty-seven strings, nine languages, hand
translated, because safety text is never machine-mangled. The answer itself is
localized, not just the frame.

A guardian's phone can also unlink a child it linked. A guardian link is a
surveillance relationship that outlives its reason — children grow up, custody
changes, households end — and the surface that created it could not end it.
