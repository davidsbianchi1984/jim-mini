# ## 0.71.0: engaged sessions landed on all four clients at once
#
# `jim/engaged.py` is the online Guardian a person leaves running: open until
# they sign off, acting across their own records through a written allowlist,
# and recording the request that would take each act back.
#
# It arrived here in the same round as the console rather than a round later,
# and the reason is the feature's own bargain. Full-act autonomy is defensible
# *because* every act is listed with the way back beside it. A shell that could
# open a session and speak into it, but could not show the undo trail, would
# have taken the permission and dropped the condition it was granted under.
# All fifteen or none.
#
# Six of the fifteen are `DELETE` routes that are also doors somebody should
# always have had: until this round nothing in this product could delete a
# journal entry, a check-in, a goal or a habit. Those are worth having on a
# phone on their own account, and they landed with the screen that made them
# necessary.

# ## 0.72.0: the Studio is console-first, and this is the debt that says so
#
# `jim/widgets.py` gives somebody a box to run their own code in — no network,
# one directory, no other programs, finite time — and the seven routes below
# are its doors. They landed on the console this round and not here, which is
# a decision rather than an oversight, and the decision is recorded rather
# than left implicit because "held at zero" was a real posture and this file
# is no longer at zero.
#
# The reason is the shape of the thing. A widget is written, not tapped: it is
# a name, a body of JavaScript, and a JSON input, edited over several passes.
# A phone keyboard is the wrong instrument for the first draft of a program,
# and shipping a cramped editor on three shells to make a number stay at zero
# would have been building for the record rather than for the person.
#
#     asked     can every client reach every route
#     mattered  is each client the right place for this one
#
# The reading half that argument never excused — open it, run it, read the
# answer from the phone in a pocket — landed on this shell, as the rows that
# stood here promised. What remains is the writing half: the first draft of a
# program is desk work, and the editor's doors stay with the editor, on the
# console, alongside the delete that ends a widget's life where its
# revisions are shown.
